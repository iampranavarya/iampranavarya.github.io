NAB 2026 Recap: The Honest, Filtered Version

By Pranav Arya · PAFP · #nab · #editing · #news · #gear

NAB wrapped this week, and true to form, the volume of announcements vastly outpaced what's actually going to change anyone's day-to-day workflow in the near term. A filtered recap of what I think genuinely matters, separated from what I expect to quietly underdeliver once independent testing catches up to the keynote demos.

What I think holds up

Adobe's color management updates in Premiere, which I wrote about last week after early testing, remain the announcement I'm most confident actually changes daily workflow for editors handling mixed camera sources. On the hardware side, a few camera manufacturers previewed sensor and processing improvements aimed specifically at better computational stabilization in smaller bodies, which tracks with what I noted from CES earlier this year as a trend worth watching rather than a one-off announcement.

What I expect to underdeliver

Several AI-assisted editing demos this year showed impressively smooth automated rough cuts from raw footage, and I'd bet real money the actual shipped versions require more manual cleanup than the stage demo implied, because that's been the consistent pattern for this exact category of announcement for several years running now. Impressive on a curated demo reel with clean, cooperative footage. Messier and less reliable on the genuinely chaotic multi-source footage most of us actually work with.

  • Color management and interchange improvements: real, worth adopting
  • Computational stabilization previews: a trend worth tracking, not urgent yet
  • Automated AI rough-cut demos: expect a gap between demo and shipped reality

The broader pattern this year's show reinforced

Boring interoperability and color management fixes keep quietly mattering more to actual daily work than the flashier AI-forward keynote moments, a pattern that's held for a few years running now regardless of how the show gets covered in the broader tech press. I'll keep filtering NAB news through that lens going forward, and I'd recommend the same skepticism to anyone reading show coverage looking for what will actually change their workflow versus what will just make a good headline.
