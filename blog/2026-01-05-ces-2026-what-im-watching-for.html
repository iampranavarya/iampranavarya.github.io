CES 2026: The Three Categories I'm Actually Watching

Ahead of CES 2026, a filmmaker's short list of what's actually worth paying attention to versus the usual concept-device noise.

Pranav AryaJanuary 5, 2026 · 1 min read

CES kicks off this week, and the annual flood of concept devices that never ship is already starting to fill up my feed. I've learned to filter hard for this show specifically, because the signal-to-noise ratio is genuinely worse here than almost any other tech event I follow. Three categories I'm actually paying attention to this year, and why.

Smart glasses, round three or four at this point

Rokid's been previewing a lighter, screen-less style of AI glasses ahead of the show, which is an interesting bet against the display-heavy direction most of the category has been chasing. I want to see whether that's a genuine design philosophy or a stopgap while display tech catches up to what a lightweight frame can actually support. Battery life claims from any manufacturer at this category get an automatic skeptical read from me until independent hands-on testing exists.

Camera sensor and computational photography announcements

The genuinely interesting camera news at CES rarely comes from the camera brands themselves anymore, it comes from sensor manufacturers and computational imaging companies showing off what's coming eighteen months out. That's the stuff worth actually reading closely, because it previews what next year's action cameras and phones will quietly build on, well before any specific product announcement.

  • Smart glasses: watching for real battery numbers, not marketing claims
  • Sensor and computational photography news: often more predictive than the flashy product reveals
  • Home AI and robotics: mostly noise for my specific work, skipping most of it

What I've learned to just ignore

Concept vehicles, home robots, and anything with "the future of" in its own press release get a hard pass from me at this point, not out of cynicism exactly, just years of watching this specific category of announcement never turn into a product I could actually buy or use. I'll write up whatever actually matters once the show wraps and the concept-device fog clears.
