The Keyboard Shortcuts That Actually Save Time, Not the Full List

By Pranav Arya · PAFP · #editing · #keyboard-shortcuts · #workflow · #tutorial

Every editing app ships with a keyboard shortcut cheat sheet listing well over a hundred commands, and trying to memorize the whole thing is a waste of effort most editors, myself included for years, put in without a real payoff. A much smaller set of shortcuts accounts for the vast majority of actual time saved on a real editing day.

Trim shortcuts matter more than almost anything else

Ripple trim and roll edit shortcuts, used constantly rather than reaching for a mouse to drag an edit point, are where I've measured the biggest real speed difference in my own editing. This is the single highest-frequency action in any edit, adjusting where a cut lands, and doing it without leaving the keyboard compounds across hundreds of small adjustments in a way that adds up to real hours over a project.

Marker shortcuts, underused by most editors

Dropping a marker with a single key while scrubbing through footage, rather than stopping to click a marker button, means I can flag good moments during a first watch-through without breaking momentum. That first-pass momentum matters more than people give it credit for, stopping to click through a menu breaks your read on the footage in a way that a single unconscious keystroke doesn't.

  • Ripple and roll trim shortcuts: the highest-frequency, highest-value habit
  • Single-key marker drops during first watch-through
  • J-K-L variable speed scrubbing, still underused by a lot of editors

Why I stopped trying to memorize everything

Chasing shortcut mastery for rarely-used commands, obscure export presets, niche panel toggles, has a real cost in time spent learning something that saves seconds a few times a year. I'd rather deeply internalize the dozen shortcuts I use hundreds of times a day and look everything else up as needed. That's a more honest use of the limited time anyone actually has to build muscle memory.
