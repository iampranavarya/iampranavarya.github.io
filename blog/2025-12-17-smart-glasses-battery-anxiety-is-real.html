Smart Glasses Battery Anxiety Is a Real Design Problem

By Pranav Arya · PAFP · #smart-glasses · #meta · #wearables · #review

Months into wearing smart glasses regularly on shoots, the single biggest thing holding them back from genuine daily reliance isn't the camera quality or the AI features. It's a specific, low-grade anxiety about battery level that I didn't fully anticipate and that I don't think manufacturers have solved yet, regardless of what the spec sheets claim.

The problem is invisible until you actually rely on the device

A phone's battery anxiety is a solved cultural problem, everyone carries a charger, everyone knows their phone's rough daily pattern. Smart glasses battery anxiety is different because the device is on your face, not in your pocket where you can glance at a percentage constantly, and running out mid-event means losing a capture capability at exactly the moment you might need it most, with no easy way to top up without taking them off and finding a charging case.

How I've actually adapted my behavior

I've started treating the glasses' camera as a bonus capture layer rather than a primary tool specifically because of this anxiety, using them for opportunistic quick clips early in an event when battery is fresh and consciously not relying on them for anything critical in the back half of a long day. That's a workaround, not a solution, and it means the device isn't actually delivering on the "always available" promise that's central to its whole pitch.

  • Battery anxiety is a real, underdiscussed adoption barrier for wearables
  • A charging case in the bag isn't the same as always-available capture
  • Using the device for early-event opportunistic shots hedges the risk

What would actually fix this

A meaningfully longer runtime, doubling current battery life rather than incremental ten percent gains generation over generation, is the only thing I think actually solves this rather than just delaying it. Fast wireless top-ups from a phone in a pocket, without removing the glasses, would help too. Until one of those actually ships, I'll keep treating these as a nice extra rather than a device I fully depend on.
