AI Video's Best Use Case Might Be the Pitch, Not the Final Cut

By Pranav Arya · PAFP · #ai-video · #client-work · #pre-production · #workflow

The place AI video generation has become genuinely load-bearing in my pipeline isn't where most coverage of the technology focuses. It's not final delivery, at least not primarily. It's the pitch stage, showing a client a rough visual direction before they've committed budget to a real shoot, and that shift has changed how I win and structure projects more than any single generated deliverable has.

Why a pitch-stage mockup changes the conversation

Describing a concept to a client verbally, or even with a mood board of reference images, leaves real room for misalignment that doesn't surface until you're deep into an expensive real shoot. A rough AI-generated sequence showing the actual pacing, mood, and general visual direction closes that gap early, when a misunderstanding costs an afternoon of regeneration rather than a wasted shoot day and a frustrated client.

What this changes about the actual proposal process

I now build a short generated concept reel into most pitches for anything above a certain budget, not as the final product, explicitly framed as a rough direction the client is approving before real production begins. It's added a step to proposals, and it's also meaningfully reduced the revision cycles after actual shoot days, because the alignment happened earlier and cheaper, before real cameras and crew were booked.

  • Rough AI concept reels catch misalignment before an expensive real shoot
  • Frame it explicitly as direction approval, not a preview of final quality
  • Revision cycles after real shoot days have measurably dropped since adopting this

Where I'm careful about this

The framing has to be honest with clients about what's generated concept versus what the final delivered footage will actually look like, because setting an expectation with polished AI output and then delivering real photography that looks meaningfully different creates its own kind of disappointment. A clear conversation about that gap upfront has kept this from becoming a problem so far, and I'd rather over-communicate that distinction than let a client assume the pitch reel is the product.
