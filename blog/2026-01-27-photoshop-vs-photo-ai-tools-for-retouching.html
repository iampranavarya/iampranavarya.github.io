Where Photoshop Still Beats the New AI Retouching Tools

AI retouching tools handle a lot of quick fixes well, but there's a specific category of edit where manual Photoshop skill still wins.

Pranav AryaJanuary 27, 2026 · 1 min read

AI-assisted retouching tools have gotten good enough that I use them constantly for quick, high-volume work, blemish removal, basic skin smoothing, background cleanup on a batch of images with no time for manual attention. There's a specific category of edit where I still reach for manual Photoshop work every time, and it's worth being honest about where that line actually sits.

Where AI retouching genuinely wins

Repetitive, well-defined fixes across a large batch, removing a consistent distraction from dozens of frames, basic frequency separation style skin work, background object removal on simple backgrounds, AI tools handle this faster than manual work and the quality is close enough that clients never flag a difference. For volume work on a deadline, this is a real, unambiguous win over doing it by hand.

Where manual work still wins clearly

Anything involving genuinely complex lighting reconstruction, removing an object that requires rebuilding shadows and reflections convincingly around where it used to be, still needs a human eye and manual cloning and healing work. AI fill tools guess at what should be there based on surrounding pixels, and that guess breaks down fast in anything with directional light, reflective surfaces, or a complex background pattern that needs to continue believably.

  • High-volume, well-defined fixes: AI tools are faster and good enough
  • Complex lighting and reflection reconstruction: still needs manual skill
  • Hero images for a campaign: always get a manual quality pass regardless

The workflow that actually works best

I run AI tools first as a fast first pass even on hero images, then manually review and fix whatever it got wrong rather than starting from scratch. That hybrid approach is faster than pure manual work and more reliable than trusting AI output blindly on anything client-facing. The skill that's actually become more valuable isn't manual retouching itself, it's knowing quickly where an AI pass is going to fail before you even run it.
