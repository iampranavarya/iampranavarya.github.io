AI Video Tools Are Growing Up: Why Security and GDPR Now Matter to Your Brand

As AI video moves into enterprise marketing, security audits and GDPR checks become part of choosing tools. What European brands should ask before uploading assets.

Pranav AryaNovember 17, 2026 · 2 min read

A year ago, AI video tools were mostly toys for creators. In 2026 they became infrastructure for marketing teams. Higgsfield, one of the platforms I use regularly, said in late September that it had crossed $1 billion in annualized revenue, extended its funding round with TCV and NVIDIA in October, and on November 11 announced that it had completed a SOC 2 Type II audit and an independent GDPR assessment.

That last announcement is less exciting than a new model, but it may matter more to European brands. It reflects a shift in who uses these tools and what they expect.

Why this matters

When a brand commissions AI video, it usually hands over sensitive material: unreleased products, packaging, campaign plans, sometimes photos of real employees or customers. All of that is uploaded to a platform to generate content. Brands in the EU should care about where that data goes, who can access it, and whether it is used to train models.

  • SOC 2 Type II is an independent audit of how a company handles security over a period of time, not just on paper.
  • GDPR compliance covers how personal data is processed, stored and transferred, which matters as soon as real people appear in your references.

Neither guarantees that nothing can go wrong, but they are the questions an enterprise procurement team will ask, and increasingly so will mid-sized brands.

Questions to ask before uploading brand assets

  • Is my uploaded material used to train the provider's models? Can I opt out?
  • Where is the data stored, and is it transferred outside the EU?
  • Who owns the generated output, and what are the commercial use terms?
  • Can I delete my uploads and generations, and how quickly?
  • Does the provider have independent security audits?

Real people need extra care

If your AI project uses photos of real people, such as a founder, staff or customers, you need their consent for that use. Under GDPR, a person's face is personal data. Get written consent that covers AI processing and the specific campaign, and keep it on file.

How we handle client assets

On client work, we only upload what a shot needs, use reference material the client has the right to use, and keep a record of which tools were used for each project. When a brand has strict rules about where data may go, we plan the production around those rules from the first call, not after the fact.

The tools are growing up quickly. Brands should grow up with them, and treat AI production with the same care as any other supplier that handles their data. If you want to talk through how an AI campaign would work with your compliance requirements, start with our AI video production page.
