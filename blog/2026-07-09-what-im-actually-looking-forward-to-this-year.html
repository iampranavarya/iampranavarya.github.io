What I'm Actually Looking Forward to in Gear This Year

By Pranav Arya · PAFP · #gear · #wearables · #opinion · #cameras

Rather than another speculative prediction list, I want to write down what I'm actually looking forward to in gear over the coming months, based specifically on what would change real production work rather than what makes for an exciting headline.

Meaningfully better battery life across every wearable category

More than any single flashy feature, genuinely doubled battery life on smart glasses and similar wearables would do more to change how I actually use this gear day to day than another camera resolution bump or another AI feature layered on top of an already-capable device. Battery anxiety has been the single most consistent limiting factor across everything I've tested in this category over the past year, and I'd trade a lot of other feature improvements for real progress here specifically.

Continued consolidation in AI video model consistency

The reference-anchored consistency improvements that made campaign-scale AI generation genuinely usable this year still have real room to improve, particularly for multi-subject scenes, and continued progress there would open up categories of work that still require real photography purely because generated consistency hasn't been reliable enough yet. That's a less flashy hope than a headline-grabbing new capability, and it's the one that would actually change my day-to-day pipeline the most.

  • Real battery life improvements across wearables, not incremental gains
  • Continued multi-subject consistency progress in AI video and image generation
  • Small, boring workflow and interoperability fixes over flashy new features

Why I've stopped chasing the flashiest announcements

A year of closely tracking gear and AI tool releases has taught me that the announcements that actually change my work are rarely the ones that dominate a keynote or a launch-day headline. They're the quieter, unglamorous fixes to genuinely limiting problems, battery life, consistency, interoperability, that rarely get the same coverage but matter far more to what actually gets delivered to a client at the end of a real working day.
