What Backstage Fashion Week Actually Teaches You About Speed

By Pranav Arya · PAFP · #fashion-photography · #technique · #event-photography

Backstage at fashion week is genuinely one of the hardest environments I shoot in regularly, terrible mixed light, constant movement, zero control over blocking, and a hard deadline before each look walks out. Shooting it repeatedly over the years has taught me more about working fast under real pressure than almost any other recurring job on my calendar.

Pre-decide your settings, don't chase perfect exposure live

There's no time to carefully meter a scene backstage, light changes constantly as models move between different fixtures and mirror stations. I set a manual exposure and white balance baseline for the general space before things get chaotic and accept some frames will be slightly off rather than chasing perfect exposure shot to shot and missing moments while adjusting. A slightly imperfect exposure I actually captured beats a perfect one I missed while dialing in settings.

Anticipate, don't react

By the time you see a genuinely great moment happening backstage and react to it, it's often already over, hands moving too fast, a stylist's adjustment complete before the shutter fires. Watching for the setup to a moment, a stylist reaching for a specific tool, a model's expression shifting as they check a mirror, and firing slightly ahead of the actual peak moment has gotten me far more usable frames than trying to react in real time ever did.

  • Set exposure and white balance for the space, don't chase perfection live
  • Anticipate the moment building, don't wait to react to it happening
  • Shoot in bursts during genuinely fast action, single frames during calmer beats

Why this training transfers everywhere else

The instinct to pre-decide technical settings and anticipate rather than react has genuinely improved my work in far calmer environments too, brand shoots, controlled portraits, situations with plenty of time to think. Once anticipation becomes a habit rather than a scramble, it doesn't switch off just because the environment got easier. Backstage fashion week is a brutal training ground, and I've come to appreciate what it built in me even on the days I dread shooting it again.
