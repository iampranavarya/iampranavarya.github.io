Prompt Libraries Are Overrated. Here's What Actually Works Better

By Pranav Arya · PAFP · #ai-video · #prompts · #workflow · #opinion

Prompt libraries and template collections are everywhere now, shareable documents full of "proven" prompts for specific looks and shots. I collected a fair number of these early on and have mostly stopped using any of them, not because they don't work, but because relying on them turned out to actively slow down developing the actual skill that matters more.

Templates optimize for someone else's exact scenario

A prompt that produced a great result for someone else's specific subject, lighting, and camera move was tuned to that exact combination, and copying it for a different subject rarely transfers as cleanly as the sharing culture around these libraries implies. I'd spend real time adapting a borrowed template to my actual need, time that, in retrospect, would have gone further spent understanding why the original prompt worked well enough to write my own from scratch.

What actually builds real prompting skill

Writing prompts from a blank page, failing, adjusting, and noticing the specific pattern that fixed a specific problem, built genuine transferable understanding far faster than collecting other people's successes ever did. It's slower in the short term and produces someone who can actually solve a new prompting problem on the spot rather than someone with a large folder of templates that only work for scenarios someone else already solved.

  • Borrowed templates optimize for someone else's exact scenario, not yours
  • Blank-page practice builds transferable skill faster than template collecting
  • Keep personal notes on why something worked, not just what the prompt said

Where I still think shared prompts have real value

As a source of ideas about what's even possible, a shared prompt is genuinely useful, seeing an unfamiliar technique named and demonstrated can open up an approach I wouldn't have thought to try. The mistake is treating that idea as a finished tool to copy-paste rather than as inspiration to understand and then rebuild in your own words for your own actual shot. That distinction, inspiration versus template, has made the biggest difference in how fast I've actually improved.
