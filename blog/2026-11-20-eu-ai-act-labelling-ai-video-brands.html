AI Labels Are Now Law in the EU: A Practical Guide for Brands Using AI Video

The EU AI Act's transparency rules apply since August 2. What brands publishing AI video need to label, what counts as a deepfake, and practical steps.

Pranav AryaNovember 20, 2026 · 2 min read

Since August 2, 2026, the transparency rules in Article 50 of the EU AI Act apply. Many brands still are not sure what that means for their AI video. With AI content now part of almost every campaign, here is a practical summary from a production point of view. It is not legal advice; for your specific case, talk to a lawyer.

Two sets of obligations

  • Providers, the companies that build generative AI systems, must mark their output in a machine-readable way, such as watermarks and metadata, so it can be detected as AI-generated. Systems that were already on the market before August have until December 2 to adapt.
  • Deployers, the people and companies who use AI to create content professionally, must visibly disclose deepfakes. That includes brands and agencies publishing AI video.

Purely personal use is outside the scope. Professional use, including marketing, is not. And the rules reach content shown to people in the EU, wherever the company is based.

What counts as a deepfake

Under the rules, a deepfake is AI-generated or AI-manipulated image, audio or video that resembles real people, objects, places or events and could falsely appear authentic. In practice:

  • A realistic AI video of your real founder saying something: label it.
  • A photorealistic AI scene presented as real footage of your actual hotel or product: label it.
  • An obviously fantastical scene, like a person flying unaided, is generally not considered a deepfake.
  • For work that is clearly artistic, creative, satirical or fictional, a lighter disclosure that does not spoil the work is enough.

Penalties under this part of the law can reach €15 million or 3% of global annual turnover.

Practical steps for brands

  • Keep a simple log. For every campaign, note which assets were AI-generated or AI-edited, and with which tools.
  • Agree on a standard label. A short line such as "Made with AI" in the caption or an on-screen tag. Consistency is easier than deciding every time.
  • Use platform labels. TikTok, YouTube and Meta all offer AI content labels. Turn them on when they apply.
  • Do not strip metadata. Provider watermarks and content credentials exist for a reason. Keep them intact in your exports where possible.
  • Be extra careful with real people. Get consent for any likeness, and label anything that could be mistaken for real footage of them.

Labelling does not hurt good work

Some brands worry a label makes their content look cheap. In my experience the opposite is true: audiences respond to quality and honesty, and a well-made film labelled as AI says the brand is modern and transparent. On our own website, AI films are clearly marked as made with AI, right next to our real shoots.
