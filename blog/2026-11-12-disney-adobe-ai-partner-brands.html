Disney Picks Adobe as Its AI Partner. Why Brands Should Pay Attention

Disney named Adobe its Official AI Innovation Partner, with custom Firefly Foundry models. What it signals about commercially safe, brand-trained AI.

Pranav AryaNovember 12, 2026 · 2 min read

On November 9, at the start of Adobe MAX, Adobe and The Walt Disney Company announced a multi-year strategic alliance that makes Adobe Disney's Official AI Innovation Partner. Disney gets early access to Adobe's newest AI and creative tools, including Firefly, Premiere and Photoshop, plus Firefly Foundry: custom AI models built with Adobe's engineering and research teams. Adobe also joins StudioLAB, the Walt Disney Studios innovation hub, where the first projects focus on custom models for post-production and visual effects. ESPN's innovation centre is part of the deal too.

The interesting thing is who Disney picked. Not one of the big general AI labs, but the company that already runs most of the world's creative pipelines.

What this tells us

Large studios have two problems with AI. The first is legal: they need to know what a model was trained on and that its output will not create rights problems. The second is identity: a studio with a recognisable look cannot use a model that produces generic images. Custom models, trained for a specific company and plugged into the tools its artists already use, address both.

Disney's past attempts to bring AI into its work have attracted criticism, so it is notable that the partnership is framed around supporting artists inside existing workflows rather than replacing them.

Why this matters for regular brands

You do not need Disney's budget to take the same lessons:

  • Ask where your AI content comes from. For paid campaigns, know which models were used and what their commercial terms say. Some tools are built and marketed specifically as commercially safe.
  • Protect your look. The more AI content there is, the more generic it becomes. Your brand's references, colours, characters and products should be built into every project, not left to the model's defaults.
  • Keep humans in the loop. The pattern at the top of the industry is AI inside creative teams, not AI instead of them.
  • Think in systems, not one-offs. Disney is investing in a long-term setup. Brands that produce content every month benefit from a consistent pipeline with saved references and rules.

Custom models will come down-market

Today, a fully custom model is a studio-scale project. But the same idea already exists in lighter forms: reusable characters, saved style references and trained product elements on creative platforms. Within a year or two I expect most serious brands to have their own reusable AI assets, the same way they have brand guidelines today.
