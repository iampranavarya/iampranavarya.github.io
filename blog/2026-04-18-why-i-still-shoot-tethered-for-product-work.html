Why I Still Shoot Tethered for Product Photography

By Pranav Arya · PAFP · #tethering · #product-photography · #workflow · #technique

Wireless transfer and instant cloud preview have gotten good enough that tethered shooting can feel like an old-fashioned holdover, a cable running from camera to laptop in an era where everything else went wireless. For product photography specifically, I still tether on essentially every job, and it's not nostalgia, it's still genuinely the fastest and most reliable option available.

Reliability under real studio conditions

Wireless transfer, even the good current implementations, introduces a real possibility of a dropped frame or a lag spike at exactly the wrong moment, a client watching a live feed during a critical shot. A physical tether doesn't have that failure mode, and for product work where a client is often watching the live feed and calling shots in real time, that reliability difference matters more than the minor inconvenience of a cable.

Instant, full-resolution preview beats a compressed wireless stream

A tethered feed shows the actual captured file at full resolution immediately, letting a client zoom in and check genuine sharpness and detail on the spot rather than trusting a compressed wireless preview that might hide a focus miss until later review. Catching a soft focus or a stray reflection in real time, on the actual file rather than an approximation of it, has saved reshoots more times than I can count.

  • No wireless dropout risk during a critical client-watched capture
  • Full-resolution instant preview, not a compressed wireless approximation
  • Direct-to-folder file organization happens automatically, no separate import step

Where wireless genuinely makes more sense

For anything requiring real physical movement, event coverage, anything handheld, tethering obviously isn't practical and wireless transfer or no live transfer at all is the right call. The distinction that matters is whether the shoot is a controlled, stationary setup or a mobile one, and product photography sits firmly in the stationary category where a cable's reliability advantage clearly outweighs its inconvenience.
