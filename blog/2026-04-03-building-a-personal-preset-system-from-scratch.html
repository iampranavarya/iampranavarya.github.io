Building a Preset System From Your Own Work, Not a Download

By Pranav Arya · PAFP · #lightroom · #presets · #photo-editing · #tutorial

After years of buying and eventually abandoning other people's preset packs, I built my current preset library entirely from my own past edits, images I'd manually adjusted and genuinely liked the result of, rather than from a downloaded pack built around someone else's photos and someone else's taste. The process was slower to start and has held up far better over time.

Start from edits you already made, not a blank slate

Rather than building a preset from scratch and guessing at what adjustments might look good, I went back through a year of delivered work and pulled out the handful of edits I still genuinely liked months later, the ones that held up rather than the ones that felt right in the moment and aged poorly. Each of those became the basis for a preset, reverse-engineering the settings from a result I already knew worked rather than guessing forward toward one.

Build narrow, purpose-specific presets, not one universal look

My library isn't one signature look applied everywhere. It's a handful of narrow presets each built for a specific lighting condition or subject type, an outdoor daylight preset, a mixed indoor event preset, a low-light preset, because a single universal preset built from one lighting condition predictably fails on everything else, the same problem with off-the-shelf packs I wrote about before.

  • Reverse-engineer presets from your own past edits that held up over time
  • Build narrow, condition-specific presets rather than one universal look
  • Revisit and update presets periodically as your own taste actually shifts

Why this has held up better than any pack I bought

A preset built from your own genuine taste and your own actual shooting conditions fits your real work in a way a generic pack, built to look good on someone else's sample photos under someone else's lighting, structurally can't. It took real time to build this way instead of downloading a finished pack in five minutes. A year later I'm still using nearly the entire library, which was never true of anything I bought.
