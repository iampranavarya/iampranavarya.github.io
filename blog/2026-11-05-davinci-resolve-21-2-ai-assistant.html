DaVinci Resolve 21.2 and the AI Assistant Moving Into Your Edit Bay

Resolve 21.2 adds touch support for the new MacBook Pro, Gen 6 LUTs and faster M5 rendering, building on 21.1's AI assistant. What matters for working editors.

Pranav AryaNovember 5, 2026 · 2 min read

Blackmagic Design released DaVinci Resolve 21.2 on November 4, as a free update for both the free version and Studio. It is a point release, but it lands in the middle of a bigger shift: Resolve is becoming a place where AI assistants work alongside editors, not just a toolbox with AI buttons.

What is new in 21.2

  • Touch support for Apple's new MacBook Pro with the PROLED XDR touchscreen.
  • Blackmagic Generation 6 colour science LUTs and film looks.
  • Up to 5x faster effects rendering on M5 Pro and M5 Max machines, according to Blackmagic.
  • Multicam clips that open as source clips in source timelines, compound clip changes and EXIF metadata in PNG files.
  • Further AI assistant refinements in the paid Studio version.

Resolve Studio is also 20% off at $236 until November 20, which is worth knowing if you have been running the free version and hitting its limits.

The bigger story: the assistant from 21.1

The real change arrived in September with Resolve 21.1, which added AI assistant integration, including a native MCP server. In plain terms, that lets an external AI assistant talk to Resolve and carry out actions inside your project. Adobe has announced a similar direction for Premiere, so this is where the industry is heading.

What does that mean in practice for an editor? The promising use cases are the boring ones:

  • Organising media, renaming clips and building bins from a shoot.
  • Finding every shot of a specific person or product across hours of footage.
  • Setting up timelines, deliverables and render presets for ten aspect ratios.
  • Applying the same correction to dozens of clips that share a camera and scene.

Those tasks eat hours on every event and campaign edit. Handing them off frees time for the work clients actually pay for: story, pacing and colour.

Where I would be careful

  • Project safety. Anything that can change a project can damage one. Duplicate your project before letting any assistant work on it.
  • Client media and privacy. Check what an assistant sends outside your machine, especially with unreleased client footage.
  • Creative decisions. Let the assistant prepare the edit, not make it.

Should you update?

If you are on a new M5 Mac or want the Gen 6 LUTs, yes, after finishing any running project. As always, do not update Resolve in the middle of a client job; project files can change with updates. Finish, archive, then update.
