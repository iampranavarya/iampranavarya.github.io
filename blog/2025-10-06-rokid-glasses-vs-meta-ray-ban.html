Rokid Glasses vs Ray-Ban Meta: Two Very Different Bets

By Pranav Arya · PAFP · #rokid · #meta · #smart-glasses · #comparison

Rokid's glasses launched in China earlier this year and are rolling out more broadly now, and they make an interesting contrast with the Ray-Ban Meta line I've been using on shoots. Both get lumped into "smart glasses" coverage, but they're built around genuinely different bets about what people actually want on their face.

Different core problem, different design

Meta's glasses are camera-first. The AI assistant and audio features are real but secondary to the fact that you're wearing a wearable action camera that looks like normal sunglasses. Rokid is betting on a heads-up display and spatial AR overlay as the primary feature, with the camera playing a supporting role. If you're trying to capture moments hands-free, Meta's approach is more mature right now. If you want information overlaid on your actual field of view, Rokid is further along on that specific problem.

Where Rokid actually surprised me

The display brightness and outdoor visibility on Rokid's current hardware held up better than I expected in direct testing outside, which has historically been the weak point of any waveguide display. It's still not something I'd trust for critical framing decisions on a paid shoot. As a quick reference display, checking a shot list or a translated sign without pulling out a phone, it's genuinely useful in a way that feels closer to finished than experimental.

  • Meta Ray-Ban: better for capture, more mature camera pipeline
  • Rokid: better for information overlay, earlier but more ambitious AR
  • Neither replaces a real camera or a real monitor for professional work yet

Why I'm watching both, not picking one

These aren't really competing products despite the category label. I could see a version of my kit in a couple of years running a capture-focused pair for quick POV footage and a display-focused pair for on-set reference, the same way I already carry both a wide and a tele lens instead of expecting one lens to do everything. For now, both stay in the experimental drawer rather than the main kit, useful for specific moments rather than daily reliance.
