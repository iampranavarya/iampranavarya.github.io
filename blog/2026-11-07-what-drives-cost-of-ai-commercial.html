What Actually Drives the Cost of an AI Commercial in 2026

AI video generation costs cents per second, yet AI commercials still cost real money. Where the budget goes, and how to brief so you pay for the right things.

Pranav AryaNovember 7, 2026 · 2 min read

One of the most common questions we get from businesses is simple: if AI video costs a few cents per second to generate, why does an AI commercial cost more than that? It is a fair question, and the answer says a lot about where the value in AI production actually sits.

The raw generation really is cheap

Public pricing makes the point. Vidu's new Q4 preview starts at $0.014 per second of video. Runway's Gen-4.5 works out to roughly $0.12 per second. Even premium models rarely cost more than a few dollars for a usable clip. If a commercial were just one generation, it would cost less than lunch.

But a finished commercial is never one generation.

Where the budget really goes

  • Concept and script. The idea that makes a brand memorable. This is the most valuable part of any commercial, AI or not.
  • Look development. Building the brand's world: references, colours, characters, products. This is what makes the film look like you and not like everyone else using the same model.
  • Iteration. For every shot in the final cut, there may be dozens of generations. Most are close but not right: a hand looks off, a logo warps, the light does not match the previous shot.
  • Consistency work. Keeping the same product, character and lighting across every shot is still the hardest technical problem in AI video.
  • Edit, colour and sound. The same craft as a traditional commercial: pacing, grade, music, sound design and mix.
  • Versions. Vertical, square, widescreen, several hooks for ad testing, language versions.
  • Usage rights. Where and how long the film runs, especially for paid media.

Why it is still far cheaper than a shoot

Even with all of that, an AI commercial removes the most expensive parts of traditional production: location fees, travel, crew days, equipment rental, permits, catering and reshoots when the weather turns. For products, places and concepts, that typically brings the total down dramatically compared with a comparable live-action commercial. Some AI platforms claim around a tenth of traditional production cost; the real ratio depends heavily on the brief.

How to brief so you pay for the right things

  • Bring good references. Clear product photos, brand guidelines and examples you like save iteration time.
  • Be clear about where it runs. A six-second bumper and a ninety-second brand film are different jobs.
  • Approve the script before production. Changes are cheap on paper and expensive after generation.
  • Ask for a campaign, not a clip. One concept cut into several versions and hooks gives far more value than one isolated film.
  • Share a budget range. A good studio will tell you what is realistic inside it rather than guessing.
