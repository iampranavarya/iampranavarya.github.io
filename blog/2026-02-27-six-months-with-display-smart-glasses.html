Six Months With Display Smart Glasses: The Honest Verdict

By Pranav Arya · PAFP · #smart-glasses · #meta · #review · #wearables

Six months of near-daily use of display-equipped smart glasses is enough time to move past first-impression enthusiasm and give an honest verdict, the kind that accounts for the days I forgot to charge them and the features I genuinely stopped using versus the ones that became real habits.

What became a genuine daily habit

Quick reference glances, a shot list, a translated sign, a text message I don't want to break eye contact with a client to check, have become a real, unconscious habit rather than a novelty I have to remind myself to use. That's the actual test for whether a piece of technology has earned a permanent place: not whether it impressed me on day one, but whether I reach for it without thinking about it six months later.

What never became a habit despite good intentions

The camera capture feature, which I expected to use constantly for quick opportunistic clips, ended up used far less than expected, mostly because the battery anxiety I wrote about a couple months back never fully resolved, and I subconsciously started saving battery for reference features rather than spending it on capture. That's a genuinely useful piece of self-knowledge about how I actually prioritize a limited resource under real conditions.

  • Quick reference glances: became a real, unconscious daily habit
  • Opportunistic capture: underused, battery anxiety won out unconsciously
  • Outdoor display visibility: still the weakest point in bright direct sun

The honest verdict

These have earned a permanent spot in my kit, just for a narrower set of uses than the marketing pitch implied when I first bought them. Not a camera replacement, not even primarily a capture device for me despite my early expectations. A genuinely useful reference and communication tool that quietly removes small friction throughout a shoot day. That's a smaller win than the hype promised and a real one nonetheless.
