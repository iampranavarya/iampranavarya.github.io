Teleprompter Setups That Actually Work for a Solo Shooter

By Pranav Arya · PAFP · #teleprompter · #solo-shooting · #gear · #tutorial

Most teleprompter advice assumes a dedicated operator scrolling text and watching for a talent's pace, which doesn't help much when I'm both the camera operator and, occasionally, the person delivering scripted lines with nobody else on set to run the prompter manually.

Voice-triggered scrolling changed this more than any hardware upgrade

Software that scrolls based on detecting your own speech pace rather than a fixed timed scroll has been the single biggest improvement for solo teleprompter use, removing the need for a second person or an awkward foot pedal setup entirely. It's not flawless, a stumble or a long pause can throw off the pacing briefly, but recovering from that is far easier than the alternative of fighting a fixed-speed scroll that doesn't care whether you just fumbled a line.

Physical setup matters more than the software

A compact prompter attachment that sits directly in front of the lens, rather than a separate screen off to the side, keeps eye line convincingly close to the camera even when I'm also handling framing and focus myself. Off-axis prompter placement is the most common mistake I see in other solo-shot content, a subtle but noticeable eye line drift that immediately reads as someone reading rather than speaking naturally.

  • Voice-triggered scrolling removes the need for a second operator
  • Keep the prompter directly in front of the lens, not off to the side
  • Practice a read once before rolling, even voice-triggered scroll benefits from familiarity

What still requires an actual second person

For anything with genuinely complex blocking, walking while delivering lines, interacting with a product while reading, I still bring in a second person to run things manually, because the cognitive load of operating camera, framing, and delivery simultaneously has a real ceiling. Solo teleprompter work is genuinely solved for static, simple deliveries. It's not a full replacement for a real crew on more complex setups, and pretending otherwise leads to a worse result than just booking the help.
