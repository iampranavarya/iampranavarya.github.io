Adobe MAX 2026: What Actually Matters for Video Editors

Adobe MAX 2026 brought Premiere's Color Mode out of beta, AI audio tools, generation inside Premiere and agent support via MCP. The filtered version for editors.

Pranav AryaNovember 10, 2026 · 2 min read

Adobe MAX 2026 opened in Miami Beach this week, and as expected almost every announcement involved AI. Most of the coverage focuses on Firefly and the new agent features. For people who edit video for a living, here is the filtered version: what ships now, what is still beta, and what is worth your time.

Shipping now in Premiere

  • Color Mode is out of beta. Introduced at NAB earlier this year, it lets you grade without leaving the editing workspace. For many editors doing their own colour on social and event work, this is the most practical update of the show.
  • AI audio cleanup. Better tools for fixing dialogue recorded in noisy rooms, which is most event and interview footage.
  • Generation inside Premiere. Video, music and sound effects generation are moving into the editor, so you can create a missing shot or a sound effect without switching apps.

Coming later: agents in your project

The more significant long-term news is that Adobe is adding support for third-party AI agents through MCP integrations, which will let outside agents work with native project files from early 2027. Blackmagic added a similar capability to DaVinci Resolve in September. In other words, both major editing platforms are opening up to AI assistants that can carry out tasks inside your project.

That is not available yet, so there is nothing to change in your workflow today. But if you run a team, start thinking about which repetitive tasks you would hand to an assistant: media organisation, versioning, captions, deliverables.

Firefly and the rest

  • Firefly keeps growing as a hub. Adobe now offers a large set of AI models, including partner models, inside its apps.
  • Firefly Design and Firefly Graph are in beta, with a private beta Firefly Builder for interactive prototypes. Interesting for designers, less relevant for editors right now.
  • Premiere on iPhone now includes a large library of tracks from major labels, but for personal, non-commercial videos only. Do not use those tracks in client work.

Adobe also announced partnerships with Google and Disney at MAX. The Disney deal deserves its own discussion, because it says a lot about where commercially safe AI is heading.

What I would do this week

  • If you grade in Premiere, try Color Mode on a real project and see if it replaces your round trip to another app for simple work.
  • Test the audio cleanup on your worst interview recording from this year.
  • Treat generated shots and sounds inside Premiere like any AI asset: check rights, check the details, and label where required.

As usual, do not update mid-project. Finish, archive, then update.

Whatever editor you use, the craft that matters is the same: story, pacing, colour and sound. That is what we deliver on our brand films and AI commercials.
