Which Action Camera Firmware Updates Are Actually Worth Installing

By Pranav Arya · PAFP · #action-camera · #firmware · #gear · #workflow

I got burned once by installing a firmware update the night before a paid shoot, only to discover a new bug affecting a feature I actually needed the next morning, and it taught me a more disciplined approach to firmware updates that's served well since. Not every update deserves immediate installation, however good the changelog sounds.

Never update the night before a paid job, full stop

This is the single rule that's prevented the most stress: any firmware update, no matter how minor or how good the changelog reads, waits until there's genuine buffer time to test it before it needs to perform on a real job. A new bug discovered with zero time to roll back or work around it is a far worse outcome than running slightly outdated but known-reliable firmware for one more shoot.

Read the changelog for what actually changed versus what's marketing

A lot of firmware changelogs bury the actually meaningful changes, bug fixes for specific reported issues, stability improvements for specific features, under more prominent language about minor new features most users won't notice. I now specifically look for bug fix language relevant to features I actually use before deciding an update is worth the risk of installing, rather than updating reflexively just because a new version is available.

  • Never install untested firmware the night before a paid shoot
  • Read for genuine bug fixes to features you use, not just new feature marketing
  • Test thoroughly with real footage before trusting a new version on a real job

The exception: security and critical stability fixes

For firmware explicitly addressing a critical stability issue, something causing crashes or corrupted files rather than just adding a feature, I'll update sooner even with less buffer time, because the risk calculation flips: staying on known-buggy firmware becomes the bigger risk in that specific case. That's a genuine exception to the general rule, not a contradiction of it, and knowing the difference has kept gear reliable through years of regular firmware churn.
