Why the DJI Mini 4 Pro Is Still My Travel Drone Pick

By Pranav Arya · PAFP · #dji · #drone · #gear · #travel

I get asked constantly whether it's time to move up to a bigger, more capable drone for brand work, and I keep landing on the same answer: no, not for the kind of shoots I actually run. The Mini 4 Pro's sub-250 gram weight class isn't a compromise I'm tolerating, it's the entire reason the drone gets used as much as it does.

Weight class is a workflow decision, not a spec

Under 250 grams means simpler registration requirements in a lot of the countries I travel to for work, and it means I can throw the drone in a backpack without a second thought about airline weight limits or dedicated case space. A heavier drone with genuinely better sensor performance sits at home more often than it flies, because the friction of bringing it is higher. A drone that isn't in the air on the day you need it is worth nothing, regardless of spec sheet.

Where image quality actually matters versus where it doesn't

For establishing shots, reveals, and the kind of sweeping brand-film opener that's become almost a cliché of its own, the Mini 4 Pro's sensor is genuinely good enough that clients have never once flagged a difference from a larger drone's footage. Where it shows limits is tight, controlled product-style aerial work in mixed or low light, and honestly that's a small fraction of the shots I actually need from a drone in a given year.

  • Sub-250g class removes real friction, and friction kills usage
  • Good enough sensor for the majority of establishing and reveal shots
  • Bigger drones still win for tight, controlled, low-light aerial work

The obstacle avoidance trade-off

Omnidirectional obstacle sensing on this class of drone has closed the gap with heavier professional models more than most specs on the sheet, and that's the upgrade that's actually changed how confidently I fly it in tight event spaces. I still fly conservatively around crowds regardless of what the sensors claim. No amount of avoidance tech changes what happens if I'm wrong about a gap.
